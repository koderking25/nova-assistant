A voice for your Mac

Say its name.
Consider it done.

Nova,

Nova is a free assistant that lives on your Mac and listens for one word. It opens your apps and works inside them, plays your music, answers questions, remembers things for you, and teaches piano. You can use all of it without looking at the screen.

Download for Mac See what it does The code on GitHub

Free and open source. Version 1.0. macOS 13 or later, Apple silicon and Intel. A 4 MB download.

Like talking to someone

No commands to learn.

Say it the way you would say it to a person. Nova works out what you mean, does it, and tells you what actually happened. If it is not sure, it asks. If something fails, it says so.

Nova, start an audio track in Logic.

Done: New Audio Track, in Logic Pro.

Nova, play Island in the Sun by Weezer.

Playing Island in the Sun, by Weezer.

What key is this in?

Let me look that up. It is in G major, at about 115 beats a minute.

Nova, remember that the recital is on Friday.

I'll remember that.

What it does

One word, and your Mac listens.

Everything below works by voice. Most of it works by keyboard and by piano keys too.

Apps

Works inside your apps

Nova reads an app's own menus and buttons, finds the one you mean, and presses it. It asks before anything that deletes, closes or sends.

"In Logic, new audio track, and then open the mixer"

Music

Plays what you ask for

Songs, artists and albums on Spotify. Anything on YouTube. Skip, pause, shuffle, and ask about the song that is playing.

"Play Harry Styles on YouTube"

Answers

Looks things up

The time, the weather, tomorrow's forecast, sums and conversions. With the optional Claude link it searches the web and tells you the answer aloud.

"Do I need a jacket tomorrow?"

Memory

Remembers for you

Notes, shopping and to do lists, alarms, reminders, focus sessions, and a morning briefing that pulls them together.

"Add milk to my shopping list"

Files

Finds your files

Ask what is new in Downloads, open a folder, or find a file by name. Nova only reads and opens. It never moves or deletes.

"What was that file in Downloads?"

Screen

Tells you what is there

Where you are, what the window says, which buttons you can press. Then press one by name, or dictate text into the app in front.

"Read the window" then "Click Create"

Yours

Answers to the name you choose

Nova, Aria, Luna, Atlas, Orion, or a word of your own. Change it whenever you like, just by asking.

"Change your name to Luna"

Close the window and Nova keeps listening. A small glowing panel rises when it hears its name, and slips away when it is done.

Built for every body

Designed to work without the screen.

Accessibility is not a setting that was added later. It is where the design started, so blind, low vision, deaf and one handed users get the whole app, not a smaller one.

  • Nothing needs sight. Every action is spoken, and every result is said aloud.
  • Nothing needs hearing. Everything said is shown as large captions that light up word by word, with colour cues for right and wrong.
  • Nothing needs a mouse. Click buttons, choose menus and type text with your voice.
  • One hand is enough. Piano lessons can be right hand only or left hand only.
  • Your pace. "Talk slower", "repeat that slowly", larger captions and high contrast.
  • Works with VoiceOver. Every control is labelled, and Nova never talks over its own captions.

If you play

A music room inside your Mac.

Plug in a piano keyboard and Nova becomes a teacher, a band and a notebook. No piano? Everything else still works.

Learn

Lessons by ear

Nova plays a short part, names the notes, then listens while you play it back. Calm feedback, no nagging, and it always says when you got it right.

Compose

Never lose an idea

Just play. Nova tells you the key and chords, saves it, and plays it back with a bass, strings and cello written to fit what you played.

Jazz

A jazz course

Nine lessons on swing, the blues scale and improvising, with a walking bass and drums to play along with.

Play

Music made for you

"Play me something sad." Nova writes a new piece on the spot and performs it. Turn your own piece into a lullaby or a film score.

Train

Ear training and a metronome

Find the note by ear, keep time with a click you can see as well as hear, and run timed practice sessions.

See

A visualiser

Your playing as rising light, filling the window. Or watch any MIDI file fall onto the keys while it plays.

Two minutes

Install it.

  1. Download

    Get Nova.zip and open it. Drag Nova into your Applications folder.

  2. Open it the first time

    Nova is new and Apple has not checked it yet, so the first double click is refused. Open System Settings, go to Privacy and Security, scroll down, and choose Open Anyway next to Nova. On older versions of macOS you can instead hold Control, click Nova, and choose Open. You only do this once.

  3. Say hello

    Nova asks a few easy questions: your name, what to call it, your city for the weather, your birthday if you want a greeting on the day, and how it can fit you. Every screen has a skip button.

  4. Allow what you want

    The microphone lets it hear its name. Accessibility lets it press things in other apps. Each is asked for once, and Nova works without the ones you decline.

Download Nova

Works on its own. Optional extras: the Spotify app for music on Spotify, REAPER or Logic Pro for recording, and Claude Code for open questions and web answers.

Private by design

What you say stays with you.

An assistant that is always listening has to earn that. Here is exactly what Nova does.

  • Speech is turned into text on your Mac, by macOS itself.
  • Nothing is acted on unless it follows the assistant's name.
  • What it overhears is never shown and never saved.
  • Your name, notes, lists and music stay in a folder on your Mac.
  • No account. No sign up. No tracking. No ads.
  • The weather sends only your city name. Song and video searches send only the words you asked for.
  • Say "stop listening" and the microphone is off.

Questions

Good to know.

Is it really free?

Yes. There is nothing to buy, no account and no subscription.

Why does my Mac warn me when I open it?

Apple shows that warning for any app it has not checked. Nova has not been through that process yet. Opening it with Control click and Open, once, tells your Mac you trust it.

Is it open source?

Yes. All of the code is on GitHub under the MIT licence. Read it, change it, build it yourself, or send improvements back.

Does music on Spotify and YouTube work?

Yes. For Spotify you need the Spotify app installed and signed in, and your Mac will ask once whether Nova may control it. YouTube opens in your browser and needs nothing extra.

Which recording software does it work with?

REAPER and Logic Pro today. REAPER gets the deepest control. Logic Pro is worked through its own menus, in English. More DAWs are coming.

How do I get the natural voice?

Say "install the natural voice", or choose it from the Nova icon in the menu bar. It is a free download of about 400 MB and runs on your Mac. Say "remove the natural voice" to take it off again.

Do I need a piano?

No. The assistant works on its own. The lessons, the band and the composing tools appear when a MIDI keyboard is plugged in.

Does it need the internet?

Listening, speaking, apps, files, notes, alarms and the piano all work offline. The weather, Spotify, YouTube and web answers need a connection.

Can I change the wake word?

Yes. Choose one during the welcome, or say "change your name to" and a new word at any time. One clear word of three to twelve letters works best.

What is the Claude link?

If you already use Claude Code and are signed in, Nova hands it the questions its own rules cannot answer, and can search the web. Without it, Nova tells you plainly when it does not understand.

Which languages does it understand?

English, for now.

How do I remove it?

Quit Nova and drag it to the Bin. Its data is in the Nova folder inside Library, Application Support, in your home folder.

Open source

Nova is free and always will be. Every line of it is public under the MIT licence, so anyone can read exactly what it does with the microphone, build it themselves, or make it better.
